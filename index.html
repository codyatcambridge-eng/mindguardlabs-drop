The machine

The Job-Recovery System

One loop from missed call to Google proof — not eight disconnected toys. Tap a step for the breakdown — what happens, why it matters, what gets wired, and how it feeds the next link (so the demos below make sense).

What happens
A homeowner rings your shop line while you’re on a roof, in an attic, or driving. The call goes unanswered or to voicemail — and most people never leave one.
Why it matters
Speed-to-lead still decides who gets the job. If a competitor answers first, you’re bidding on leftovers. This is the leak Install Intensive is built to plug — and what Demo A shows.
What gets wired
A missed-call trigger on your shop line (Make.com) that fires the moment a call is missed — no phone pick-up required from you.
Feeds next
That trigger hands the lead to step 2: an auto SMS text-back in seconds, so the conversation starts without you climbing down.
What happens
Within seconds of the miss, the homeowner gets a shop-branded text: you’re sorry you missed them, and you’re ready to help today or this week. The thread lives in Messages — not buried in voicemail.
Why it matters
This is the recovery moment. Demo A’s “After — Run” path is literally this step: miss → SMS firing → conversation. Without it, step 1 is just a lost call.
What gets wired
Missed-call → SMS scenario, your wording, your shop number, and a qualify prompt that invites them to say what they need.
Feeds next
Their reply (job type + timing) becomes the input for step 3 — qualify and book an estimate window without phone tag.
What happens
You (or the scenario) confirm a Thursday 2pm-style window. An estimate chip lands where you can see it — calendar / Jobber / your board.
Why it matters
Phone tag kills momentum. Booking from the SMS thread keeps them warm while you’re still in the field.
What gets wired
Qualify prompts + estimate chip path inside Intensive — same flow you watch end Demo A.
Feeds next
A booked window means you show up with context ready to send an estimate (step 4).
What happens
Quote goes out while context is fresh — on site or rolling to the next call.
Why it matters
Estimates that sit in a notebook for three days lose to shops that text a number tonight.
What gets wired
Intensive covers the recovery path into an estimate; Team Agents can draft follow-ups and crew notes around it.
Feeds next
Silent quotes need a polite chase — step 5’s Day 3 / 7 / 14 sequence.
What happens
Nudges go out on Day 3, 7, and 14 — and stop the moment they reply so you don’t look like a spam bot.
Why it matters
Most “lost” jobs were never lost — they were quiet. A clean sequence recovers them without you remembering who to chase.
What gets wired
Follow-up scenario in Intensive; Care keeps it healthy over months.
Feeds next
When they say yes and the work gets done, you’re into job complete (step 6) with proof ready.
What happens
Job wraps. You’ve got a photo and a happy customer — the raw material for reviews and social proof.
Why it matters
Without a capture habit, proof dies in the camera roll and the next homeowner who looks you up sees an empty grid.
What gets wired
Social Engine board + Team field-report path turn “job done” into assets you can approve once.
Feeds next
Proof photo + timing feed step 7 — review ask and multi-platform posts (Demo B).
What happens
Timed review ask while they’re happiest, plus an approve-once post that can hit IG / FB / Google — Demo B’s Social Engine board.
Why it matters
After a quote, homeowners check your profile on IG and Google. Empty grid = distrust. Alive grid = you look like the shop that actually works.
What gets wired
Social Engine ($1,997): caption pipeline, platform toggles, calendar chips. Review ask starter can begin in Intensive.
Feeds next
Closes the loop: the next homeowner who checks your profile after a quote sees proof — which makes step 1–3 easier next time.

I already have Jobber / ServiceTitan — why you?

Those run the job. We plug the leak before the job exists — missed-call SMS, follow-up that stops on reply, and social that keeps proof alive after the estimate. They stack; they don’t replace each other.

Who owns the Make.com scenario?

You do. We build in your account. You keep the logins — you type passwords. We leave you with a working scenario you can trigger without us.

How long until it works?

Install Intensive: half-day. Social Engine and Team Agent Stack: typically 1–2 weeks to live. You trigger Intensive once without us before we leave.

Do I need to understand AI?

No. You need to answer texts and approve posts. Grok and Hermes are assistants with memory and handoffs — not something you prompt from scratch every day. Hermes needs tuning early on until the board matches how you run.

Is Social Engine in the $497?

No. Intensive covers missed-call recovery and follow-up. Social Engine ($1,997) is a separate custom board — approve → multi-platform. Ask if you want both.

Is NovaBloomChat part of Install Intensive or Team Agents?

No. Nova ($39) is a separate private on-device Mac AI product. It does not include social automation or the Hermes team stack. Hands-on shop install is still Install Intensive / Install Day.

Remote vs Newnan / Coweta on-site?

Both. Coweta-area shops can get on-site Intensive. Remote works for the same installs over screen share. You still type your own passwords either way.
